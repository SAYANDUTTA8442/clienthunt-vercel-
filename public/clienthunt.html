Competition overview

A business problem, then a build

ClientHunt is a competition organized by Freelanthropy. It is not an open-ended ideathon: every team receives the same real-world business problem and works from it.

The rounds follow the path of a real client engagement: understand the brief, respond professionally, price the work, then deliver within the client's limits. In Round 1, teams work online. They study the problem, shape a solution, complete the Case Study and Professional Mail Writing components, and prepare their own project quotation.

Eligible teams are shortlisted for Round 2, an offline product-development challenge. There, the Organizing Team provides a budget, and teams build within that budget and a three-hour limit.

  • Understand a real business problem
  • Analyse requirements
  • Develop an appropriate solution
  • Communicate professionally
  • Estimate project costs
  • Build under budget and time constraints

Step by step

Your Journey at ClientHunt

  1. Team Registration

    Teams register with 1–4 participants.

  2. Business Problem Release

    The Organizing Team shares the common real-world business problem.

  3. Round 1 Submission

    Teams complete all four Round 1 components and submit their work.

  4. Evaluation

    Online submissions are evaluated.

  5. Shortlisting

    Eligible teams are shortlisted and notified through email.

  6. Offline Round 2

    Shortlisted teams attend the three-hour product-development challenge.

  7. Departmental Evaluation

    Web Development and Graphics Design independently evaluate their respective submissions.

  8. Final Results

    The Organizing Team consolidates the scores and publishes the results.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is ClientHunt?

    ClientHunt is a two-round competition in which teams analyse a real-world business problem, develop a solution, submit a project quotation and, if shortlisted, build a product in a three-hour offline round.

    Who organizes ClientHunt?

    ClientHunt is organized by Freelanthropy — Wings of Arthniti, under STC, IIT Patna (Hybrid). It is a competition run by Freelanthropy, not a separate organization.

    What is the team-size limit?

    Teams may have 1–4 participants.

    What kind of problem will be provided?

    The Organizing Team provides a real-world business problem. All teams work from the same problem; it is not an open-ended choose-your-own-idea format. The problem is shared after registration.

    What are the Round 1 components?

    Round 1 has four components: Business Problem Analysis & Solution Development, Case Study, Professional Mail Writing and Project Quotation.

    What is the Case Study component?

    The Case Study is a separate Round 1 component. Its exact deliverable and submission format will be defined in the official rulebook.

    What is the purpose of the Round 1 quotation?

    Teams prepare their own quotation estimating the cost of their proposed solution. It is part of Round 1 evaluation and shows your commercial thinking.

    Is the Round 1 quotation the Round 2 budget?

    No. The Round 1 quotation is prepared by your team. The Round 2 budget is provided by the Organizing Team, and your quotation does not determine it.

    Who provides the Round 2 budget?

    The Organizing Team provides a defined budget for Round 2. Teams plan resources and develop their product within it.

    How long is the offline challenge?

    Round 2 is a three-hour offline challenge for shortlisted teams.

    How are Round 2 submissions evaluated?

    Two departments, Web Development and Graphics Design, each evaluate their respective submissions independently.

    How are final results published?

    After both departments complete their evaluations, the Organizing Team consolidates the scores and publishes the final results.

    How will shortlisted teams be notified?

    Shortlisted teams are notified by email.

    Can submitted team details be modified?

    Not directly. Team details are locked after registration. Request any correction through the Organizing Team; requests are reviewed and may be approved or rejected.

    Where can participants read the official rules?

    In the Official Rulebook.

    Ready to Take the Challenge?

    Bring your team, analyse a real business problem, prepare your quotation and demonstrate your ability to execute within constraints.

    Team size: 1–4 participants
